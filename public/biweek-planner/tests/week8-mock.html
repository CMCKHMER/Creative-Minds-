Reading Passage

For thousands of years, people have observed objects in the night sky and created names for them. Ancient civilizations such as the Greeks, Romans, Egyptians, and Babylonians connected celestial objects with their languages, beliefs, and traditions. Some names were associated with mythology, while others described recognizable patterns of stars. As astronomy developed, scientists needed more systematic ways to identify celestial objects. Groups of stars that formed recognizable patterns became known as constellations. Only some individual stars, especially bright or important ones, received separate names. Later, some stars were named after astronomers associated with their discovery or study. Today, astronomers use numbers, scientific designations, or locations in the sky. Organizations such as the International Astronomical Union establish official naming practices, while commercial services that sell star names do not necessarily provide names recognized by the scientific community.
