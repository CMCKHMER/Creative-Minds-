Grammar
Reported Speech
Reported Statements
When we report what someone said, we usually move the tense back one step.

Said + (that) + subject + past tense
Direct: "I train every day."
Reported: He said (that) he trained every day.

Direct: "We won the game."
Reported: They said (that) they had won the game.
Reported Questions
For yes/no questions, use if or whether.
For WH-questions, keep the question word.
Use statement word order (no question mark).
Direct: "Are you coming to the match?"
Reported: She asked if I was coming to the match.

Direct: "Where did you buy those shoes?"
Reported: He asked where I had bought those shoes.
Reported Commands & Requests
Use told + person + to + verb for commands.
Use asked + person + to + verb for requests.
Direct: "Pass the ball!"
Reported: He told me to pass the ball.

Direct: "Please help me train."
Reported: She asked me to help her train.
Common Changes in Reported Speech
Pronouns: I → he/she, my → his/her
Time: today → that day, now → then, yesterday → the day before
Place: here → there, this → that
"I am tired today." → She said she was tired that day.
"We will meet here." → They said they would meet there.
"I saw him yesterday." → He said he had seen him the day before.
Important: If the reporting verb is in the present tense (says, tells), the tense does not change.

✅ She says she loves basketball. (no change)
✅ She said she loved basketball. (tense backshift)
Pronunciation: Stress in Reported Speech

When reporting what someone said, the main stress usually falls on the reporting verb (said, told, asked) or on the new information.

"She SAID she was tired."
"He ASKED if I wanted to play."

Reading
The Spirit of Kung Fu

🥋 The Spirit of Kung Fu

At the Shaolin Temple in China, young monks begin their training before the sun rises. They run up steep stone steps, practice kicks until their legs shake, and meditate in complete silence for hours. To outsiders, this life looks extreme. But for the monks, kung fu is not just a martial art — it is a way of life.

The word "kung fu" does not actually mean "fighting." It means skill achieved through hard work. A master chef has kung fu. A talented musician has kung fu. At the Shaolin Temple, the monks apply this idea to everything they do. They believe that true strength comes from the mind, not just the body.

"When I first arrived, I wanted to learn the most impressive moves," says Liu Wei, a 22-year-old monk who has trained at Shaolin for eight years. "But my master told me, 'Before you can break a board, you must learn to stand still.' I spent my first year only practicing basic stances. I was frustrated. Now I understand — the basics are everything."

Shaolin kung fu has existed for over 1,500 years. During that time, it has survived wars, political changes, and the modern world. Today, the temple is both a religious site and a global attraction. Tourists come to watch performances, and thousands of students from around the world come to train. Some stay for a few weeks. Others, like Liu Wei, dedicate their entire lives to it.

The monks follow a strict daily schedule. They wake at 4:30 a.m., practice until breakfast, study Buddhist philosophy, train again in the afternoon, and meditate before bed. There is little free time and no smartphones. "It is difficult," Liu Wei admits, "but when you remove distractions, you discover what you are truly capable of."

Not everyone who studies kung fu becomes a monk. Many students return to normal life after their training. But they carry the spirit of kung fu with them — the discipline, the patience, and the understanding that mastery takes time. "Kung fu teaches you that limits exist mostly in your mind," Liu Wei says. "When your mind is strong, your body will follow."

Writing
Write About Important Qualities
Writing Strategy — Writing a Timed Essay:
1. Read the prompt carefully and identify the topic and task.
2. Plan for 5 minutes: Brainstorm ideas and create a quick outline.
3. Write for 20–25 minutes: Follow your outline. Include an introduction, body paragraphs, and a conclusion.
4. Save 5 minutes for revision: Check grammar, spelling, and organization.
5. Stay focused on the topic — do not include unrelated information.

Essay Prompt

What are the three most important qualities of a successful athlete? Choose three qualities and explain why each one is important. Give examples from sports, your own experience, or the reading in this unit.

  1. Introduction: Introduce the topic and state your three qualities.
  2. Body Paragraph 1: Explain the first quality + example.
  3. Body Paragraph 2: Explain the second quality + example.
  4. Body Paragraph 3: Explain the third quality + example.
  5. Conclusion: Restate why these qualities matter and end with a final thought.

Model Paragraph: The Qualities of a Champion

In my opinion, the three most important qualities of a successful athlete are discipline, mental toughness, and humility. First, discipline is essential because talent alone is not enough. As the Shaolin monks demonstrate, mastery comes from repeating basic skills thousands of times. Second, mental toughness allows athletes to perform under pressure. Maya Torres, the tennis player, said that she still feels nervous before every match — but she has learned to control her fear and stay focused. Finally, humility is important because it helps athletes learn from failure. A humble athlete listens to their coach, respects their opponent, and always looks for ways to improve. In conclusion, while physical ability opens the door to sports, it is discipline, mental toughness, and humility that turn a good player into a great one.

Speaking
Present an Activity
Exercise A — Prepare your presentation

Choose a sport, game, or physical activity that you enjoy. You will present it to the class for 2–3 minutes. Use the outline below to prepare.

Presentation Outline

  1. Introduction: Name the activity and why you chose it.
  2. Basic Information: How many people play? What equipment do you need? Where do you do it?
  3. Rules or Skills: Explain one or two important rules or skills.
  4. Personal Experience: When did you start? Who do you play with? What do you enjoy most?
  5. Conclusion: Invite your classmates to try it.
Speaking Strategy — Speaking from an Outline:

Do not memorize every word. Instead, memorize the main points and speak naturally.
Use transition words: "First," "Next," "For example," "In conclusion."
Make eye contact with different people in the audience.
Practice your opening and closing sentences — these are the most important.
If you forget something, pause, take a breath, and move to the next point. The audience will not know you skipped something.
Useful Language for Your Presentation

Introducing: "Today I want to talk about..." / "The activity I have chosen is..."

Describing: "You need... to play this sport." / "The most important skill is..."

Sharing experience: "I started playing when I was..." / "The best part is..."

Concluding: "I really recommend this because..." / "If you want to try it, you should..."

Exercise B — Peer Evaluation

While your classmates present, listen and evaluate using these criteria.

The speaker introduced the activity clearly.
The speaker explained the rules or skills well.
The speaker used personal experience and examples.
The speaker spoke clearly and made eye contact.

Expanding Your Fluency
The Sports Interview Challenge
Scenario: The Press Conference

Work in groups of four. One person is a famous athlete who just won a major championship. The other three are journalists. The journalists interview the athlete, then report what the athlete said to the class using reported speech.

🏆 Athlete:
You just won an Olympic gold medal. Answer the journalists' questions. Be creative! Talk about your training, your feelings, your opponents, and your future plans.
🎤 Journalists:
Prepare 3–4 questions each. After the interview, take turns reporting what the athlete said. Use reported speech correctly.

Example: "I asked her how she felt, and she said that she was the happiest person in the world."
Question ideas for journalists:
• How long have you been training for this moment?
• What did you think when you realized you had won?
• Who helped you the most on your journey?
• What are you going to do next?
• What advice do you have for young athletes?
