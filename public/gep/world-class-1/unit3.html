Grammar
Review of Future Forms
will + base form
Use for predictions, promises, offers, and decisions made at the moment of speaking.
I think solar energy will become the main power source.
I 'll help you install the panels.
be going to + base form
Use for plans and intentions, or predictions based on present evidence.
We are going to replace our old windows.
Look at those clouds—it 's going to rain!
Present Continuous for Future
Use for definite personal arrangements at a specific time in the future.
I 'm meeting the energy consultant tomorrow at 3.
We 're having a meeting about the budget next week.
Future Continuous: will be + -ing
Use for actions that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.
This time next year, we will be living in a solar-powered home.
Future Perfect: will have + past participle
Use for actions that will be completed before a specific time in the future.
By 2030, scientists believe they will have found a way to store solar energy more efficiently.
Tip: We often use will after phrases like I think, I don't think, I'm sure, I doubt, probably, maybe. We use going to when we have already decided to do something or when we can see evidence now.

Reading
Plugging Into the Sun: Why don't we use solar energy more?

☀️ Plugging Into the Sun

Every hour, the sun beams more energy onto the Earth's surface than the entire human population uses in a whole year. So why aren't we using more of it? The answer is complicated, but the future is bright.

One major problem is storage. Solar panels only generate electricity when the sun is shining. At night or on cloudy days, the power drops. To solve this, engineers are working on better batteries. Tesla's Powerwall and similar home batteries allow families to store solar energy for later use. As battery technology improves, solar power will become more reliable.

Another challenge is cost. Ten years ago, installing solar panels was extremely expensive. Today, the price has dropped by more than 70%. In many countries, governments offer tax breaks and incentives to families and businesses that switch to solar. Some homeowners who install panels even sell extra electricity back to the power company.

There is also a space issue. Solar farms need large areas of land. Critics argue that this land could be used for farming or housing. However, new designs are changing that. Floating solar panels on reservoirs and solar tiles on rooftops mean we don't need extra land at all.

Finally, some people simply don't trust new technology. They worry that panels won't last or that maintenance will be costly. But modern solar panels are built to survive strong storms and usually come with 25-year warranties. Most systems need almost no maintenance beyond occasional cleaning.

The truth is, we are already plugged into the sun—our planet depends on solar energy for weather, plant growth, and warmth. The question is not whether we can use more solar power. We can. The question is whether we will choose to do it before climate change forces our hand.

Writing
A Persuasive Paragraph
Writing Strategy — Being Persuasive: A persuasive paragraph presents your opinion and supports it with facts, examples, and logical reasons. Your goal is to convince the reader to agree with you or take action.

Structure of a Persuasive Paragraph:

1. Topic sentence: State your opinion clearly.

2. Supporting reasons: Give 2-3 reasons with facts or examples.

3. Concluding sentence: Restate your opinion and call the reader to action.

Model Paragraph

Our school should require all students to take a course on energy conservation. First, young people are the ones who will face the worst effects of climate change, so they need information now. Second, when students learn about saving energy at school, they often teach their families at home. For example, after my cousin's school started a recycling program, her entire family began composting food waste. Finally, education is the cheapest and most effective way to create long-term change. If we want a cleaner future, we must start in the classroom.

Persuasion Strategy: Use these phrases to make your argument stronger:

• Open with impact: "Did you know that..." / "Imagine if..."
• Give evidence: "According to a recent study..." / "Research shows that..."
• Address concerns: "Some people think... but the truth is..."
• Call to action: "If we all do this, we can..." / "The time to act is now."
