02 / Read

The reading

Read each short section carefully. Notice when a source gives an estimate rather than a certain number.

Where Ghost Mountain is

Ghost Mountain, also called Phnom Khmaoch, is an area near Preah Vihear Temple in Cambodia’s northern Dangrêk Mountains, close to the Cambodia–Thailand border.

Before the tragedy

From 1975 to 1979, Cambodians suffered under the Khmer Rouge. People faced executions, forced labour, starvation, and disease. Many families had already experienced profound loss.

1979 and displacement

Early in 1979, Vietnamese forces overthrew the Khmer Rouge government. Conflict and food shortages continued. Many Cambodians fled toward Thailand. This movement away from home is called displacement.

The forced return

In June 1979, Thai authorities collected refugees from border camps and transported them to Preah Vihear. UNHCR records that more than 42,000 people were forced down the mountainside into Cambodia. A refugee is someone who flees danger and seeks safety elsewhere.

The descent

The refugees faced steep terrain and landmines, which are explosive devices hidden in or on the ground. Historical accounts report that some people were shot when they tried to return to Thailand. Others died from exhaustion, malaria, or starvation.

International response and change

The International Committee of the Red Cross appealed publicly for the pushbacks to stop. UNHCR later acknowledged that it had failed to publicly protest the mass expulsions. On 19 October 1979, Thailand announced that Cambodian refugees would receive temporary asylum—permission to stay for safety—and would not be forcibly returned.

What is known and what is uncertain

The death toll is not known with certainty. UNHCR describes at least several hundred and possibly several thousand killed in the minefields. Documentation Center of Cambodia research gives estimates of up to 3,000 killed and 7,000 unaccounted for. “Unaccounted for” means a person’s fate was not established; it does not confirm that the person died. Estimates differ between sources, and people reported missing must not be counted as confirmed deaths.

Why it is remembered as Ghost Mountain

Survivor memories describe cries for help and the deaths of people who had already escaped years of terror. “Ghost Mountain” is a remembered name that reflects grief and memory. It is an interpretation of what happened there, not a single undisputed explanation of the name.

05 / Useful words

Vocabulary box

Refugee
A person who flees danger and seeks safety elsewhere.
Forced return
Sending a person back when they do not choose to go.
Asylum
Permission to stay in a country for protection.
Landmine
An explosive device hidden in or on the ground.
Displacement
Being forced to leave one’s home or community.
Survivor
A person who lived through a dangerous event.
Unaccounted for
Someone whose fate has not been established; not a confirmed death.
Estimate
A careful approximate number when an exact number is not known.
